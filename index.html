How it works

Six steps, no admin.

1

Say what's stuck

Not "I need maths help" — "I don't get completing the square". One specific thing fits in 15 minutes.

2

Pick a free slot

The board below shows who's available and when. Choose whoever covers your subject.

3

Meet in the library

Lunch or straight after school. Bring the question, the worksheet, or the past paper.

4

Leave with flashcards

Every session ends with 3–5 cards on what you just covered, added to the shared deck below.

5

Tutors log hours

Instead of a fee, tutors bank volunteer hours — useful for references, UCAS and awards.

6

Nobody's out of pocket

No fees, no subscriptions, no "premium". If it costs money, it isn't QuickStudy.

The rest of it

Other things QuickStudy does.

The wanted board

Requests nobody can cover yet, posted publicly. It doubles as the recruitment pitch: "three people need Further Maths, can you help?"

Exam-week pop-ups

In the fortnight before mocks, slots double up and run every lunchtime. Same 15-minute format, more desks.

Hour leaderboard

Volunteer hours, visible. Not a competition exactly, but it's the only reward on offer, so it may as well be on the wall.

Deck of the week

Whichever deck got the most new cards gets printed and pinned up outside the library. Low-tech revision for people who don't open the site.

  • Run it through a teacher or head of year first — you'll need a room booking and sign-off on peer tutoring.
  • Keep sessions in public, supervised spaces. Library or a classroom with staff nearby, never empty rooms.
  • Two tutors minimum per subject so one person's absence doesn't kill the slot.
  • Tutors aren't teachers. The rule is "explain what you understand", not "mark work or hand over answers".
